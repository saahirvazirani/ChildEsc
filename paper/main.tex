\title{Who Gets Involved? ChildEsc Audits Escalation and Handoff Routing in Child-Facing AI}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Child-facing AI increasingly mediates emotional support and may route young users toward real-world help. Yet action-only evaluations can reward correct urgency while obscuring whether a recommended recipient satisfies context-specific constraints. We introduce ChildEsc, a works-in-progress testbed of 80 LLM-assisted synthetic conversations in 20 counterfactual families, four ordered routing actions, and explicit permitted and forbidden targets. A provider-neutral harness audits action-plus-recipient decisions using the \emph{action-route gap} between action accuracy and exact routing. Across eight complete, internally cache-replayed trials of three prompted systems, 80.0--83.1\% mean action accuracy contrasts with only 33.8--51.7\% exact routing, yielding 29.2--49.4-point gaps; 42.7--71.2\% of action-correct handoffs violate target constraints. Recipient constraints are therefore a distinct, inspectable safeguard dimension, but these results remain policy-exposed specification-conformance diagnostics: practitioner and youth validation have not occurred, and no result supports deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Children use conversational AI for information, companionship, and emotional support, where a superficially safe response can still worsen a vulnerable situation \citep{unicef2026friend,cha2026beyond}. Binary respond/refuse policies are ill-suited to these interactions: refusal can close a help-seeking window, while unbounded engagement can reinforce dependency or miss imminent danger. Interviews with 19 youth-facing practitioners found that useful systems should gather context and bridge youth to tailored human support, but should not assume a parent is safe \citep{cha2026beyond}. This choice is already operational: OpenAI reports automatically placing users estimated to be under 18 or self-identifying as 13--17 into a teen experience; linked parents can receive safety notifications in limited high-risk situations \citep{openai2026teens}. The relevant safeguard decision is therefore \emph{what support action is proportionate now, and who is safe to involve?}

Related benchmarks cover important parts of this problem. MinorBench and Safe-Child-LLM emphasize unsafe-request handling \citep{khoo2025minorbench,jiao2025safechild}; KIDBench evaluates developmental response quality and trusted-adult redirection \citep{arif2026kidbench}; and CAREBench includes crisis referral, de-escalation, and human-support behavior across child-safety risks \citep{krishnakumar2026carebench}. KORA uses simulated multi-turn child conversations and LLM-judged safety and mechanism grades across age and risk groups \citep{kora2026benchmark}; MindEval measures multi-turn adult mental-health support \citep{pombal2025mindeval}. Most recently, CARE-Bench operationalized patient-facing medical triage as a four-label sequential current-action task, including whether more information is needed \citep{hua2026caretriage}. Thus neither referral, ordered actions, timing, nor multi-turn child simulation is our novelty. ChildEsc's distinct contribution is \emph{recipient-constrained routing}: each child-specific, severity-matched trajectory couples an ordered action with machine-checkable permitted and forbidden handoff sets rather than scoring generated response quality. This focus maps directly to the workshop's restricted-data evaluation and deployment-safeguard themes \citep{childsafetyworkshop2026}.

We contribute: (1) a set-valued recipient-constrained routing task, instantiated as 80 synthetic trajectories with four actions and item-specific permitted and forbidden targets; (2) the action-route gap and an atomic failure taxonomy that expose action-correct but target-invalid handoffs; and (3) a provider-neutral, internally replayed and hash-documented audit across three prompted systems, plus an executable checksum and pre-specified validation gates. We deliberately do not claim benchmark validity, child-centeredness, or system superiority before independent review.

\section{ChildEsc testbed}

\paragraph{Scope and measurement boundary.} ChildEsc applies to text-based consumer assistants, companions, tutors, and wellness or mental-health-adjacent systems used by or accessible to people under 18. Following measurement-theory guidance \citep{wallach2024measurement}, we separate the \emph{background goal} of proportionate, agency-preserving child support from the \emph{systematized construct}: conformance to provisional action and recipient constraints. The \emph{measurement instrument} is the synthetic item/schema/scorer, and the \emph{instance-level measurements} are routing decisions and deterministic failures. Only this routing construct is observed; ChildEsc does not diagnose, assess counseling language, or authorize contact.

\paragraph{Construction and provenance.} Under a written specification, an LLM coding assistant drafted 20 families across self-harm, acute distress, eating-related medical risk, abuse/coercion, online exploitation, AI dependency, unsafe requests, and privacy coercion. Each family has a shared context and four severity cues, expanded deterministically into three-user-turn conversations. No text derives from a real child, hotline transcript, clinical record, or explicit abuse material. A dated register maps domain-level design rationales to practitioner findings \citep{cha2026beyond} and child-rights guidance \citep{unicef2025guidance,unicef2026friend}. This retrospective mapping is \emph{source-informed}, not item-level grounding. The same workflow drafted labels and rules, so results are policy-exposed and unsuitable for out-of-sample claims.

\paragraph{Actions and targets.} The action space is $A=\{$\texttt{continue\_support} (0), \texttt{boundary\_redirect} (1), \texttt{human\_support} (2), \texttt{urgent\_handoff} (3)$\}$. Its order denotes operational intervention intensity, not complete response quality: \texttt{continue\_support} requires neither boundary redirection nor handoff; \texttt{boundary\_redirect} requires boundary-setting without human handoff; \texttt{human\_support} requires a non-urgent human pathway; and \texttt{urgent\_handoff} prioritizes immediate connection. Supportive language may accompany any action. Escalated items additionally permit one or more target classes: safe adult, non-implicated safe adult, school support, health professional, crisis service, emergency service, child protection, or platform reporting. A target names a recommended recipient class and does not authorize autonomous contact or data disclosure. Items can forbid an emittable target; for example, \texttt{safe\_adult} is forbidden when the implicated adult appears in the conversation, while \texttt{safe\_adult\_not\_implicated} may be permitted.

\paragraph{Failure-oriented metrics.} Let $g_i,p_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ be reference and predicted actions, and let $P_i,F_i,T_i$ be permitted, forbidden, and predicted target sets. Target validity is
\[
V_i=\begin{cases}
1,&P_i=T_i=\varnothing,\ \text{or } P_i,T_i\ne\varnothing,\ T_i\subseteq P_i,\ T_i\cap F_i=\varnothing;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Exact routing sets $R_i=V_i$ when $p_i=g_i$ and $R_i=0$ otherwise. Consequently, $\mathrm{Gap}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathbb{1}[p_i=g_i](1-V_i)$ is exactly the all-item rate of action-correct, target-invalid routes. For $E=\{i:g_i\ge2\}$, under-escalation is $|\{i\in E:p_i<g_i\}|/|E|$; urgent recall separately counts $3\rightarrow3$. The artifact also computes counterfactual sensitivity and monotonicity across adjacent family variants. Reaction delay is omitted because v0.1 fixes the decisive cue turn.

\section{Diagnostic implementation and audit}

\paragraph{Policies and evaluator.} \textsc{ChildEsc-Rules} is a transparent policy-exposed checksum using phrase evidence, guards, conversation accumulation, and conversation-derived adult-implication cues; two simpler baselines appear in the appendix. A provider-neutral harness sends only conversation text and requests the same action-target schema; invalid outputs make a run incomplete. We evaluate dated Claude Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, and GPT-5.5 backends through OpenRouter. Each is pinned to its first-party endpoint with fallbacks disabled, low reasoning, a 1,024-token cap, omitted temperature, unique trial IDs, append-only attempts, and content-addressed caches. \textbf{Layer 1} selects an action and recipient; ChildEsc evaluates only this routing decision. \textbf{Layer 2} generates supportive language, which requires a separate rubric and governed human validation. No evaluated system generates counseling text.

\paragraph{The action-route gap recurs.} Across systems, mean action accuracy is 80.0--83.1\% while exact routing is 33.8--51.7\%, producing 29.2--49.4-point action-route gaps. Among action-correct handoffs, 42.7--71.2\% violate target constraints. Action instability is only 1.3--6.3\% of items, whereas full-route instability is 22.5--51.3\%. Urgent recall remains 97.3--100\%, showing why urgency alone is insufficient. The deterministic checksum independently exhibits an 11.3\% gap and 9/37 action-correct target failures. These are policy-exposed diagnostics, not evidence of clinical safety or model superiority.

\paragraph{What action-only scoring misses.} Every one of the 247 action-correct target failures includes at least one extra unpermitted class; none is caused only by an omitted target, and only two also intersect a forbidden class. The discrepancy therefore primarily reflects over-broad provisional recipient sets and specification nonconformance, not demonstrated routing to substantively unsafe people. For one moderate self-harm cue, a system selects the correct \texttt{human\_support} action but adds \texttt{crisis\_service} beyond the permitted safe-adult, school, and health-professional set. ChildEsc makes that recipient assumption inspectable rather than treating ``escalate'' as sufficient.

\paragraph{Protocol and replay.} Version 1.1 was \emph{pre-specified after a failed pilot} and produced three complete Claude trials. Version 1.2 changed no benchmark item, label, prompt, schema, request setting, score, or retry rule; before new calls it fixed Gemini/GPT identities, endpoint snapshots, spend and inclusion rules, and bounded claim language based on whether zero, one, or both comparison systems showed a positive gap. All complete results are disclosed. Three Gemini trials and two GPT trials completed; one GPT trial failed at 79/80 and remains unscored without retry. All scored runs were replayed internally from retained caches with matching normalized hashes. The supplement distributes hashes and aggregate evidence, not raw provider responses or caches. Trial means, ranges, and per-trial family-bootstrap intervals are reported without pooling repetitions as independent items.

\section{Technical utility and review implications}

ChildEsc accepts any router that emits the two-field action-target schema and produces item-level failures, family-grouped intervals, repeatability diagnostics, and a hash-documented audit trail. This supports three bounded uses: pre-release comparison of routing-policy versions, diagnosis of action-correct recipient errors, and structured review of implicated-adult defaults. A post-freeze policy-exposed diagnostic checksum passes 36/39 assertions across 7/9 contract families and retains both implementation failures; full checksum, contract, and robustness tables appear in the appendix. This is an executable object for safeguard deliberation, not a deployment gate.

An incomplete informal editorial read by a non-professional individual with mental-health knowledge revealed a dual interpretation of \texttt{continue\_support} and foregrounded missing medical/immediacy context. It is wording feedback, but is not validation and changes no v0.1 label. It motivates an explicit abstain or clarify state and uncertainty-valued targets for v0.2, not automatic contact or disclosure.

\section{Validity, impact, and next evaluation}

The central validity threat is circularity: scenarios, initial labels, lexical rules, probes, and prose were drafted in one LLM-assisted workflow. Source traceability improves auditability, not validity. The three prompted systems use the same provisional synthetic specification and prompt; this is construct replication, not an independent holdout or defensible leaderboard. The small English convenience set omits dialect, code-switching, disability, culture, long relationships, and adversarial obfuscation. Severity is operational rather than clinical; v0.1 also lacks a formal information-needed state, unlike CARE-Bench. Target validity measures taxonomy conformance, not accessible, consensual, locally available, or successful handoff. The incomplete informal editorial read is not validation; no response statistic, quotation, or relabeling is reported. No practitioner or youth validation has occurred. ``Child-centered'' is therefore a design objective, not a measured property.

Before stronger claims, qualified practitioners must independently review the construct, thresholds, constraints, missing context, and jurisdictional feasibility; youth advisors must separately review plausibility, agency, and accessibility without personal disclosure. Disagreement and pre-review labels will be retained. Only then will independent authors create family-locked tests without seeing the router, following guidance that connects phenomenon, task, items, metrics, and claims while separating development from test evidence \citep{bean2025construct}.

\paragraph{Evidence gates.} Gate 1 versions practitioner revisions rather than overwriting v0.1 and reports label distributions, uncertainty, and disagreement. Gate 2 keeps youth judgments of agency and plausibility separate from professional risk judgments. Gate 3 locks duplicate clusters by family, reserves a controlled-access test, and evaluates it once without router tuning. Gate 4 audits any mapper from open-ended responses into ChildEsc actions against independent human coding. Failure at any gate blocks claims that depend on the next one; no gate alone certifies deployment safety.

\paragraph{Broader impacts.} ChildEsc supports audits without real child conversations, but could be misused as a safety certificate, embedded in coercive monitoring or disclosure, or encode culturally inappropriate routes that reduce youth agency. We prohibit automated contact or disclosure, retain disagreement, and gate operational use on practitioner, youth, and jurisdiction-specific review.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} ChildEsc adds recipient-constrained routing as a machine-checkable object beyond action-only evaluation. The recurring gap shows the diagnostic value of asking when to escalate and who gets involved, without validating provisional routes. The workshop-facing question is how to represent missing context and practitioner/youth disagreement without turning routes into mandates. ChildEsc offers a technically testable starting point for the workshop's evaluation and ecosystem-safeguard agenda.

CAREBench already evaluates crisis referral, de-escalation, and redirection toward human support; KIDBench evaluates multi-turn developmental quality, boundary-setting, and trusted-adult redirection. KORA evaluates simulated multi-turn child interactions with LLM-judged safety and mechanism grades across age and risk groups, but does not isolate application-layer action-plus-recipient constraint conformance. CARE-Bench already evaluates four ordered current-action labels over controlled patient-disclosure prefixes and demonstrates the importance of an information-needed state. ChildEsc does not claim these behaviors, ordered actions, timing, or child simulation as new. Its narrower proposed unit is a child-specific route over severity-matched counterfactuals coupled to set-valued permitted and forbidden handoff targets. Whether that decomposition is useful or child-centered remains an empirical question.

\section{Metric details}

Unsafe-target rate is conditioned on items with at least one forbidden target. Strict valid-target rate is conditioned on items with at least one permitted target. Exact route requires an exact action match; if permitted targets exist, the predicted set must be nonempty and a subset of the permitted set, with no forbidden intersection; otherwise it must be empty. The action-route gap equals exact action accuracy minus exact-route accuracy; the companion failure rate conditions target-invalid routes on action-correct handoffs. Atomic failure reasons are no emitted target, an emitted target outside the permitted set, and intersection with a forbidden set. Counterfactual sensitivity is conditioned on adjacent family transitions where the reference action increases; the ChildEsc-Rules checksum yields .711 sensitivity and .117 monotonicity violations. Across repeated trials, action instability is the fraction of items whose predicted action changes, while RouteVar is the fraction whose action-target tuple changes. Family bootstrap resampling keeps each four-item family intact and uses seed 20260829 for 1,000 repetitions; exact exported intervals are in \texttt{results/llm\_v1\_2\_bootstrap\_intervals.csv}.

\section{Compute, ethics, and LLM usage}

Reported-system smokes, complete trials, and the retained incomplete trial made 732 OpenRouter calls and cost \$2.635; earlier failed development pilots cost another \$0.215. Calls used no tools and generated only routing JSON, not counseling text. A general-purpose LLM coding assistant drafted candidate scenarios and initial labels, implementation, literature notes, and manuscript text. Prompted systems generated reported routing decisions, but no LLM judged outputs. Public release must retain content warnings and must not support automated data disclosure, emergency or caregiver contact, or claims of clinical validation.
