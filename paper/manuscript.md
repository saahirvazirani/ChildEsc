# Who Gets Involved? ChildEsc Audits Escalation and Handoff Routing in Child-Facing AI

Anonymous workshop submission draft. The submission-authoritative source is `main.tex`; this Markdown copy is provided for collaborative editing.

## Abstract

Child-facing AI increasingly mediates emotional support and may route young users toward real-world help. Yet action-only evaluations can reward correct urgency while obscuring whether a recommended recipient satisfies context-specific constraints. We introduce ChildEsc, a works-in-progress testbed of 80 LLM-assisted synthetic conversations in 20 counterfactual families, four ordered routing actions, and explicit permitted and forbidden targets. A provider-neutral harness audits action-plus-recipient decisions using the *action-route gap* between action accuracy and exact routing. Across eight complete, internally cache-replayed trials of three prompted systems, 80.0--83.1% mean action accuracy contrasts with only 33.8--51.7% exact routing, yielding 29.2--49.4-point gaps; 42.7--71.2% of action-correct handoffs violate target constraints. Recipient constraints are therefore a distinct, inspectable safeguard dimension, but these results remain policy-exposed specification-conformance diagnostics: practitioner and youth validation have not occurred, and no result supports deployment.

## Main Paper

The complete main paper, references, appendices, source register, relational contract audit, representative examples, metric definitions, validation protocol, compute disclosure, and ethical release notes are maintained in `main.tex`. All empirical values are generated from versioned result files; `make reproduce` regenerates the testbed, metrics, error taxonomy, input-evidence ablations, orthographic checks, guard probes, contract assertions, and deterministic paper tables. The prompted-router result uses three Claude, three Gemini, and two GPT complete 80-item trials through first-party OpenRouter endpoints with fallbacks disabled. A third GPT trial failed at 79/80 and remains unscored without retry. All eight scored runs were replayed internally from retained content-addressed caches before packaging; raw provider responses and caches are excluded from the supplement. The v1.1 settings were pre-specified only after failed v1.0 pilots; v1.2 changed no benchmark item, label, prompt, schema, request setting, score, or retry rule and froze the two comparison systems and inclusion rules before its calls. These remain policy-exposed construct replications, not independent holdout evidence or a model leaderboard. ChildEsc evaluates action-plus-recipient routing only; supportive-language quality requires a separate rubric and governed human study. The incomplete informal read clarified action semantics and missing-context questions but is not validation. No practitioner or youth validation has occurred; validation materials remain prospective protocols.
