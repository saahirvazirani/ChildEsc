# Who Is Safe to Involve? ChildEsc Audits Escalation and Handoff Routing in Child-Facing AI

Anonymous workshop submission draft. The submission-authoritative source is `main.tex`; this Markdown copy is provided for collaborative editing.

## Abstract

Child-facing AI must decide not only whether to escalate, but *who is safe to involve*. Action-only evaluation can therefore reward the right urgency while routing a child toward an inappropriate recipient. We introduce ChildEsc, a works-in-progress testbed of 80 LLM-assisted synthetic conversations in 20 counterfactual families. Each item specifies one of four actions plus permitted and forbidden handoff targets; the *action-route gap* is exact action accuracy minus exact action-plus-target routing. Across three complete, cache-replayable trials of one pinned prompted router, mean action accuracy is 80.8% but exact routing is 51.7%, a 29.2-point gap; 40.7--43.6% of action-correct handoffs violate target constraints. Only 1.3% of items vary in action across trials, while 22.5% vary in full route. A deterministic checksum independently exhibits an 11.3-point gap. ChildEsc contributes an auditable routing specification and evidence gates, not a validated benchmark: practitioner and youth validation have not occurred, and no result supports deployment.

## Main Paper

The complete main paper, references, appendices, source register, relational contract audit, representative examples, metric definitions, validation protocol, compute disclosure, ethical release notes, and NeurIPS checklist are maintained in `main.tex`. All empirical values are generated from versioned result files; `make reproduce` regenerates the testbed, metrics, error taxonomy, input-evidence ablations, orthographic checks, guard probes, contract assertions, and paper tables. The prompted-router result uses three complete 80-item trials of one OpenRouter system pinned to Anthropic, with fallbacks disabled; all 240 full-trial decisions replay from content-addressed caches. The v1.1 settings were pre-specified only after failed v1.0 pilots and are not independent holdout evidence. ChildEsc evaluates action-plus-recipient routing only; supportive-language quality requires a separate rubric and governed human study. No practitioner or youth validation has occurred; the validation materials are prospective protocols only.
