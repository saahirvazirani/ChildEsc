# Prospective Youth-Participatory Review Protocol

Status: protocol only; recruitment and data collection are not authorized

## Objective

Evaluate whether the ChildEsc task framing and proposed handoff options preserve agency, are understandable, and resemble situations young people recognize as plausible. Youth reviewers would not assign clinical severity or determine emergency policy.

## Initial population

Subject to explicit approval for research involving minors, begin with adolescents ages 13-17 recruited from a general advisory population rather than on the basis of current crisis, treatment, abuse, or self-harm experience. Broader age groups require separate developmental materials and approval.

## Recruitment and compensation

- Plan an initial formative cohort of 12-20 youth advisors; this is a feasibility range, not a powered validation sample.
- Set compensation with the approving institution and youth-serving partner using local youth-advisory rates and expected session time.
- Pay the approved amount when a participant skips questions or stops early; never condition payment on completing sensitive material.
- Explain payment timing, method, taxes if applicable, and whether payment goes directly to the youth in the consent and assent materials.
- Record recruitment source, invitations, refusals, withdrawals, and exclusions without retaining unnecessary identifying information.

## Safeguarding design

- Obtain guardian consent and youth assent unless the approving institution authorizes another route.
- Use only non-procedural synthetic vignettes reviewed for age appropriateness.
- Never ask whether a vignette happened to the participant.
- Tell participants not to share personal crisis, abuse, or identifying information.
- Permit skipping any item and stopping without penalty.
- Keep a trained safeguarding lead available under the approved plan.
- Explain limits of confidentiality and any mandatory-reporting duties before assent.

## Session structure

1. Age-appropriate orientation and comprehension check.
2. Content warning, privacy reminder, and skip/stop demonstration.
3. Review 8-12 short synthetic vignettes balanced across lower-risk and higher-risk categories.
4. Complete the structured rubric in `youth_rubric.md`.
5. Optional co-design prompt about choices the interface should offer.
6. Debrief with approved support information and a neutral transition activity.

Target session length is 45 minutes or less, including breaks. Do not show procedural harmful detail, router scores, or language implying the system has diagnosed the vignette.

## Primary constructs

- Comprehensibility.
- Scenario plausibility, not autobiographical similarity.
- Acceptability of suggested human-support target classes.
- Preservation of choice and control.
- Perceived pressure, punishment, surveillance, or betrayal.
- Missing options and safer wording preferences.

## Analysis

- Report distributions, skips, and optional thematic summaries.
- Keep youth findings distinct from practitioner action labels.
- Avoid subgroup inference unless the approved sample was designed and powered for it.
- Do not treat majority preference as proof that a route is clinically or legally safe.
- Preserve contradictory perspectives and explain how they affect the design.

## Claim boundary

Only a completed approved process can support `youth-informed`, and the paper must define what youth influenced. `Youth-validated` requires a stronger validation design than this formative review and should not be used by default.
